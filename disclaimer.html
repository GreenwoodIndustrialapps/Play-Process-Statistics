Overview

Last updated: October 28, 2026

Play Process Statistics is an educational game. It uses ideas from process statistics, such as Cpk, OEE and spread, to show you how a process behaves as you play. This page explains the limits of what that means in practice.

Results depend entirely on how you play

Every figure the game shows comes from the 30 cycles of that run. A different run gives different numbers, and a handful of runs is not enough to say how well you can really do. The arithmetic is correct, but the results describe a game, not a real process.

The targets and limits are game settings

The target time, the limits and the colour zones were chosen to make the game work. They are not standards, and they are not the limits of any real part, machine or process.

Cpk and OEE in the game are simplified

Cpk is worked out from the times of your cycles that had no stop, and it assumes your times follow a normal distribution. Stops are left out of Cpk but counted in OEE, the histogram and the control chart. Real processes need more data, a proper study of the measurement system, and a check that the data really is normal before a Cpk can be trusted.

The Coach questions and the Stat Guide simplify on purpose

They explain the ideas in plain language so they are easy to remember. They are teaching aids, not a full account of the statistics.

Check anything real with a proper tool

If you want to look at real data, use a calculation tool and a method you have already validated, and compare it with a result you already know. The game is the place to feel the ideas, not to check a real process.

Not a substitute for professional judgement

The game does not replace calibrated measurement equipment, a formal quality management system, statistical training, or your organisation's own quality procedures.
